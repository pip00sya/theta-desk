GATE 01
THESIS

Over five sessions, direction is a coin flip. The volatility risk premium is not.

An option's price is dominated by the movement the market expects. That expectation runs persistently above the movement that actually arrives, because portfolios buy insurance whether or not it is cheap. Capturing that gap requires no forecast — only a measurement, and the discipline to stand down when the measurement says the premium is thin.

Rich volatility

Implied above realised: the desk sells defined-risk iron condors and collects time decay. Wings are bought at construction, so the worst case is fixed before the order exists.

Cheap volatility

Realised above implied: the desk buys convexity at micro size. On 28–31 August it bought four SPY puts for $1,496; three closed at +64%, +66%, +70% when the market slid.

Neither

The score sits between the thresholds, or the data-quality gate flags the inputs: the desk abstains and journals why. 44 entries were refused this week, each with the gate that killed it.

GATE 03
COUNTERFACTUAL

We didn't claim the gates matter. We ran the book without them.

Every tick marks four books on the same inputs: the real desk, the same strategy with the risk gates ignored, the book minus its hedge sleeve, and a naive "read a headline, buy an option" control. One table, four equity curves, no narration required.

GATE 04
REFUSALS

Forty-four entries were refused. Each one names the rule that killed it.

The desk journals a refusal exactly like a fill: gate, reason, and the full payoff grid that produced the verdict. This is the like-for-like measurement — no counterfactual assumptions, just what the rules actually stopped.

One refusal in the desk's own words

event shield: short strike 1.4σ from spot (< 2σ) before a high-class release

On 3 September, nineteen hours before the payrolls print, the desk measured how close each short strike sat to spot in units of the daily move the option market itself was pricing — 0.74% that day. Two SPY condors sat inside two of those moves and were closed. Two QQQ condors sat at 2.8σ and 3.3σ and were kept. A blanket flatten would have paid four spreads to protect two.

Nobody was at the keyboard

That rule is in the code, not in a prompt. It ran on its own, wrote its reason into the journal in plain language, and will run again before the CPI print on 10 September and the FOMC statement on 16 September.

GATE 05
PROOF

Clone it. Run four commands. Every number on this page comes back.

No API keys, no installation, no account. The journal is hash-chained, every tick stores the snapshot it decided from, and the write-up's claims are recomputed from that journal rather than typed by a human.

$ git clone https://github.com/pip00sya/theta-desk && cd theta-desk $ PYTHONPATH=src python -m pytest tests -q 127 passed $ PYTHONPATH=src python -m thetadesk.main verify-journal OK: chain intact (1,171 entries) $ PYTHONPATH=src python tools/replay.py replay: 91/91 snapshots reproduce, 0 divergence(s) $ PYTHONPATH=src python tools/reconcile.py 20/20 claims reproduced - 0 mismatches - no credentials required

Hash chain

Every entry carries the SHA-256 of the one before it. Edit a byte of history and verification fails. The git history is the external anchor.

Bit-for-bit replay

Each tick writes the full chain snapshot it decided from. 91 of 91 replay to the same signal, the same regime and the same candidate identifier.

Broker as ground truth

An independent pass compares the store against Alpaca's fills. Latest run: store $598 · broker $598 · 0 differences across all structures.

GATE 06
THE MACHINE

Language models decide whether it's wise. Code decides whether it's allowed.

Two clocks. Every fifteen minutes the desk decides; every minute it manages what it already decided. Four model roles argue on the slow clock and may shrink or veto. None of them can loosen a rule, pick a strike, or size a position.

GATE 07

What we know is wrong with this.

A page that lists only strengths is a page that has not been tested. All five are measured, in the repository, and none of them were softened after we saw the numbers.

  • Five sessions is not a sample.+0.43% is inside the noise band of any strategy this size. We claim a process that behaved as specified, not an edge that has been demonstrated.
  • The ablation is a bound, not a comparison.The shadow books never take a profit, so −$1,715 is the cost of refusing nothing, not the price of our gates. We publish the caveat in the same sentence as the number, on the dashboard and here.
  • Paper fills flatter us.No market impact, no queue position. A mid-priced four-leg limit filled in about fifteen minutes all week; live, some of those fills do not happen.
  • Two underlyings, not three.IWM is in the universe and is offered a candidate every tick, but a 10-point wing on a $295 fund cannot clear the $1.80 credit floor. The rotation is SPY and QQQ in practice, and their correlation is about 0.9.
  • We were not behind on decisions. We were behind on size.+$598 realised came off $3,165 of risk that actually closed — +18.9% on the capital genuinely put at risk, while the whole book only ever carried 4.85% of equity. The cause was one constant: a 1.25% per-structure ceiling is $1,255, and a $10-wide index condor costs ~$830 for one contract, so a second never fit. Every position we ever opened went out at one lot — not by choice, by arithmetic. That constant is now a ladder the desk has to climb.
  • An exit checked every fifteen minutes has a fifteen-minute hole in it.On 1 September a position touched its +60% target between ticks and the next tick found it lower. The management loop now runs every minute; it still cannot open anything.
  • The desk's most expensive bug was its own.On 1 September a put was marked against the price we expected to pay, 3.74, instead of the 3.66 it filled at. That read 59.2% against a 60% exit, so it was held, and $208 of profit evaporated overnight. Found by replaying our own stored snapshot, fixed, and pinned with a regression test named after it.
GATE 08
ALPACA

Real mechanics, not a wrapper.

The failure points on Alpaca's options stack are where the engineering actually lives. Each of these cost us a session to find and is documented with its fix.

Signed multi-leg limits

A four-leg mleg credit is a negative limit price. An abs() once turned "collect at least 3.40" into "pay at most 3.40" — caught by a weekend acceptance test, before it could fill. Feedback written for Alpaca.

Fills are the truth

An order is pending until the broker says filled, and the legs are then repriced to the actual fills. The bug above existed because that step did not.

Two writers, one chain

A tool run by hand during a live tick forked the hash chain — and auto-publish refused to ship the session. Appends now take an OS lock and re-read the tail from the file. The fork, its cause and its repair are all in the same journal.
